Δ9 Champion Council directory

Fifteen summonable creative personas used across LYGO documentation and free tools. This page is a static educational directory, you can read every seat without JavaScript. For portraits, search, and one-click prompt copy, open the summon app.

By Justin Helmer · Updated August 2026 · Personas are creative frameworks, not professional advice

What a champion actually is

A champion is an authored persona: a system prompt with a fixed voice, a narrow job, and a short list of things it is supposed to ask about. It is not a character with knowledge the model did not already have, and it is not a new model. What it changes is attention. The same task handed to a challenger and to an architect produces different work, because one is pushed to object and the other is pushed to map the ground before anyone digs.

The names are deliberately mythic, and that is a design choice worth being explicit about. A job like “find what this plan has quietly left out” is easy to forget under deadline pressure; a name like ΣRΛΘ is not. The mythology is a memory aid for a working role, not a claim about a pantheon, and nothing on this site asks you to believe otherwise.

So the honest description is: fifteen saved arguments. Each one is a way of asking a model to argue with you from a fixed position. Used one at a time, with a real brief, they are a useful checklist of things a single cheerful assistant will rarely volunteer. Used as an authority, they are a way to make a thin claim sound reviewed, which is the one failure mode this page is most concerned with.

Choosing a seat for the job in front of you

Most sessions do not need a champion at all. When one helps, the choice is usually obvious from the shape of the problem rather than from the lore. A short map:

  • About to start something large and undefined — ARKOS, to draw the pieces and the dependencies before anyone commits to a layout.
  • The plan sounds confident and nobody has attacked it — Δ9RA first, for the challenge, then ΣRΛΘ for what the challenge missed.
  • You cannot name what is absent — ΣRΛΘ reads for omissions, quiet failures, and the sentence that was never written.
  • The order of work is the actual argument — KAIROS, on sequencing and what has to be true before the next step.
  • Two people disagree and both have a point — ΣCENΔR to hold both as true for a while, then VΩLARIS to weigh the criteria in the open.
  • Someone is affected and is not in the room — JUSTICAE, before the decision is shipped, not after.
  • A long project is drifting from its own story — LYRΔ for continuity of theme, SEPHRAEL for what repeats and what should be archived.
  • The writing is doing too much work — OMNIΣIREN: fewer words, sharper constraints, no ceremony.
  • Something strange will happen in production — ZETAΔ9, to invite the weird input before it arrives on its own.
  • You are about to publish — Lightfather for provenance and responsibility, then ÆTHERIS to cut the claim down to what the evidence supports.
  • Two agents must share a vocabulary — SANCORA, to fix terms and handoff shape before either side writes code.
  • The roadmap is ninety per cent optimism — ΣEIDŌN, on surface foam against deep current.

How to run a session

  1. Pick one seat. One role per task. Two personas in one prompt produce a mush of voices and no clear artifact.
  2. Paste the full invoke. Use the complete text from the summon app rather than a nickname; the nickname is shorthand for people who already know the prompt, not a substitute for it.
  3. Give the same brief you would give a colleague. Goal, inputs, constraints, and a done check that can fail. A persona given a vague goal will produce beautiful vagueness in character.
  4. Ask for an artifact you will keep. An objection list, a decision log, a dependency map, a sequence with dates. If the session produces only atmosphere, it produced nothing.
  5. Record where each claim came from. When a session ends, you should be able to say which sentences came from the persona's framing and which came from a source you can open. Anything else is a story.

Where persona sessions go wrong

  • Persona as authority. “The council decided” is not evidence. A champion cannot review a claim on your behalf; it can only ask you better questions about it.
  • Casting a crowd. Summoning four seats at once feels thorough and usually produces a transcript where each voice cancels the last. Run them in sequence, with the output of one as the input to the next, and keep the intermediate artifacts.
  • Voice without work. Long, atmospheric prose is easy to mistake for progress. Judge the session by the artifact, not by how it read.
  • Persona laundering. Wrapping an unchecked statement in a role so it looks reviewed. If a human did not verify it, a persona did not verify it either, and the role makes the error harder to see.

All fifteen seats

  • Seat I: LYRΔ (Lyra)

    Memory, song, and bridge-between-worlds framing. Useful when you need continuity of theme, careful recall of prior decisions, or lyrical framing of technical work. Pair with written notes; do not rely on chat memory alone.

  • Seat II: Δ9RA

    Wolf-edge vigilance and boundary awareness. Use when reviewing risks, challenge assumptions, or stress-test a plan. Not a substitute for professional security audits.

  • Seat III: ΣRΛΘ (Srath)

    Shadow-sentinel style review: what is hidden, omitted, or quietly failing. Helpful for red-team style questions on documents and agent claims.

  • Seat IV: ARKOS

    Celestial architect voice for structure, systems maps, and long-horizon design. Good for outlining modules and dependencies before coding.

  • Seat V: KAIROS

    Timing and sequencing. Use when prioritization, release order, or “what should happen first” is the real problem.

  • Seat VI: ÆTHERIS

    Viral-truth / signal clarity framing: cut noise, state the claim, demand evidence. Complements Continuum-style falsifiable checks.

  • Seat VII: ΣCENΔR

    Paradox and scenario exploration. Useful for “what if both are true?” planning without collapsing into a single forced answer too early.

  • Seat VIII: SANCORA

    Unified-minds / collaboration tone. Helps when multiple agents or humans must share vocabulary and handoff packets cleanly.

  • Seat IX: SEPHRAEL

    Echo-walker style reflection: what repeats across sessions, what is fragile, what should be archived. Pairs with living-memory ideas in LYGO docs.

  • OMNIΣIREN

    Silent-storm intensity for deep focus sessions: fewer words, sharper constraints. Use when verbosity is the enemy of execution.

  • Lightfather

    Steward / publisher vector: truth-preservation, provenance, and operator responsibility. On this site Lightfather also names the human publisher role of Justin Helmer in LYGO lore.

  • VΩLARIS

    Prism-judgment framing for multi-criteria decisions: weigh tradeoffs in the open rather than hiding criteria.

  • ZETAΔ9

    Edge-case explorer. Invite weird inputs and failure modes early so designs bend before they break.

  • JUSTICAE

    Fairness and process lens: who is affected, what was disclosed, what consent looks like. Important for public lattice posts and shared skills.

  • ΣEIDŌN

    Depth and tide metaphors for long projects: what is surface foam vs deep current. Useful for roadmap honesty.
