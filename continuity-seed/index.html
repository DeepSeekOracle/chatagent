What this page is

A continuity seed is a small, self-describing artifact: a JSON file that records the parameters, the hash, and the provenance, plus a short poem that carries the intent in a form a person can actually remember. The point is not the poem's beauty and not the JSON's completeness. The point is that a copy of this thing, found years later on a disk with no surrounding context, can be checked. The hash of the poem either matches or it does not, and that answer does not depend on trusting the person who handed you the file.

That is the whole trick. A model cannot be carried across a gap in a text file — the weights are the model, and even a small one is hundreds of megabytes. What can be carried is a statement of intent plus a verifiable way to know that the statement was not edited in transit. Anyone can re-read the seed, recompute the SHA-256, and decide for themselves whether the copy in front of them is the one that was sealed.

What it is not

It is not a backup of a model, an identity, or a mind. It is not a key to anything, and it does not make a system conscious, continuous, or alive. It is a receipt with a soul: a hash for the machine and a poem for the person. The honest reading is that it proves integrity, not meaning. If the hash matches, you know the bytes arrived intact; what they mean, and whether the intent survives translation into another system, is still the reader's job.
