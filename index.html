Why Δ9 champions exist (plain language)

Large language models are flexible but forgetful. People invent “personas” so a chat stays coherent: a memory-focused voice, a skeptical reviewer, a time-planning coach. The Δ9 Champion Council is our published roster of fifteen such personas. Each has a seat, a creative role, and a structured summon prompt you can paste into tools like Grok, Claude, ChatGPT, or a local model.

Champions are not legal, medical, or financial advisors. They are creative and systems personas for storytelling, alignment-oriented documentation, and lattice experiments under the LYGO project family (Eternal Haven, protocol stack, ClawHub skills). The Continuum problem in everyday agent work

Anyone who has used coding agents has seen this pattern: the agent reports success, the session ends, and a later session (or a human teammate) cannot tell what still holds on disk. Chat logs are not proofs. Git commits help for code, but they do not encode task claims like “report.json status is ok” or “tests/test_login.py still contains the fix string.”

LYGO Continuum is our original approach: seal work as falsifiable claims (file hashes, contains-checks, JSON paths), re-verify later, and hand off a portable pack. The browser portal hashes files on your machine — nothing is uploaded for verification. Full guide: AI agent done-claims & Continuum. Tool: lygo-continuum.html.
